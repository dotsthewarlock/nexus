One-tap camera · 9s cutoff + Resume

Recreates the previous Nexus cropped native Play control and expands its same iframe into a camera modal. The 9-second cutoff destroys the active player. Resume starts a new, bounded viewing session.

The cropped Camera control sends your tap to Dacast's real Play button. No synthetic autoplay occurs. A focus signal is used to open the modal and begin the timer, but focus is not proof that footage is playing. Resume may still need a native Play tap.

1 · Configure

The diagnostic's Prepare action loads the original iframe beneath the Camera label, so the next tap reaches the native Play control. In the production design this preparation happens when the camera control is shown. The prepared iframe is discarded after 20 seconds of inactivity.

2 · Tap the Camera control

Camera
Not prepared. Tap Prepare, then tap Camera once.

Unlike a regular button, the white Camera face does not intercept touches. The cropped iframe underneath receives the actual tap. For keyboard access or a missed focus handoff, use the manual fallback above.

3 · Record outcomes

Use “Mark moving footage” in the modal to timestamp your own playback observation. Test first launch, cutoff, Resume, and repeated Resume cycles; the report records the session boundaries.

Event log, technical constraints

Iframe document load and focus are not playback signals. Media bytes, bitrate, source video quality and provider-internal errors cannot be inspected across origins. Each 9-second session timer uses parent-page timing. Local events are not sent anywhere.





Standalone, publicly accessible diagnostic only. No production camera, border-data workflow, player URL, credentials or operator permissions are modified.
